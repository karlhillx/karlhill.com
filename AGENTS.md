This repository represents Karl Hill's professional engineering work, leadership trajectory, and music history.

Before making substantive content changes, consult:

Treat the private context files as reference material, not website copy to reproduce wholesale. They must never be committed, quoted into public metadata, exposed through an API, or copied into client-side bundles.

## Writing voice

Use concise, direct, confident, specific language. Prefer neutral editorial copy organized around responsibility, evidence, outcomes, and technical judgment.

Avoid:

- generic portfolio language and inflated claims
- résumé-style keyword dumping
- corporate filler and buzzwords
- repeated sentence fragments
- unnecessary first-person narration
- unnecessary third-person biography language
- artificial connections between engineering and music
- claims of formal authority that the evidence does not support

## Professional positioning

Present a hands-on Staff Aerospace Software Engineer and technical delivery leader whose work connects software development, technical direction, engineering systems, delivery, and team development.

Show current scope accurately:

- software engineering remains central
- technical leadership is exercised through execution, standards, coordination, mentoring, and influence
- Agile delivery leadership spans internal and partner teams — describe the work, never name those organizations
- do not mention BlackLynx or RTX anywhere public (pages, metadata, JSON-LD, kit, resume, OG, llms, APIs)
- architecture is part of the role, but not the entire identity
- platform and DevSecOps work are meaningful strengths, but not the site's sole positioning
- Principal Engineer and Engineering Manager are future directions, not current titles

## Content method

1. Preserve strong existing copy.
2. Prefer concrete evidence over adjectives.
3. Distinguish shipped outcomes from responsibilities, proposals, and work in progress.
4. Use the smallest number of claims needed to establish credibility.
5. Flag conflicts or uncertainty instead of silently choosing the more impressive version.
6. Do not invent metrics, dates, scope, technologies, awards, ownership, or business results.
7. Keep music credible and self-contained.

## Privacy

Never infer that information in private context is approved for publication. Use only facts marked `Public`, `Public with care`, or already present in the site. Ask before publishing anything marked `Private`, `Internal`, `Unverified`, or `Needs approval`.
