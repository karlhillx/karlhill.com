The standard for what remains after the alcohol is gone.

Independent reviews of dealcoholized beer, wine, spirits, and cocktails. We review beverages at 0.5% ABV or less, and we separate products that were actually dealcoholized from those formulated to imitate a drink.

Read the reviews Editorial method

The Dry Standard concentrates on beverages at 0.5% ABV or less. We give particular weight to drinks made by removing alcohol from a finished beer, wine, or spirit — and we say so when a product was simply formulated to taste like one.

How it's made

The techniques that remove alcohol — and the ones that never put it in

Method index

Method

Vacuum distillation

Alcohol is boiled off under reduced pressure so the wine or beer never has to reach a kitchen-stove temperature.

Method

Spinning cone column

A thin-film vacuum column used to separate aroma and ethanol in stages. Common in wine industry language; less often explained on a back label.

Method

Reverse osmosis

Pressure drives the liquid through membranes that separate water and ethanol from a flavor concentrate, then the parts are reassembled without (most of) the alcohol.

Method

Membrane and cold filtration

Alcohol leaves through a filter, not a boil. Guinness 0.0 is the version of this story most people have actually tasted.
