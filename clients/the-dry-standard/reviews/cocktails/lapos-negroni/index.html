Lapo's Non-Alcoholic Negroni

Lapo's Non-Alcoholic Negroni is a Florence-born canned NA cocktail: <0.5% ABV, made from Italian ingredients to be built rather than poured straight.

Lapo's brand copy frames the product as an 'Italian-inspired cocktail' built for the NA shelf; there is no claim that the drink was ever alcoholic and then dealcoholized — the formula is built fresh.

81/100

Is it actually dealcoholized?

Lapo's brand copy frames the product as an 'Italian-inspired cocktail' built for the NA shelf; there is no claim that the drink was ever alcoholic and then dealcoholized — the formula is built fresh.

Base beverage: Italian-sourced ingredients with bitter liqueur analogues (likely sourced and built from a spirit replica or aperitivo-style formulation), no underlying Gin/Campari before dealcoholization.

Product overview

Lapo’s is born in Florence, named for a real bartender at Florence’s most famous bar — a tuxedoed figure, smiling, the cocktail done right. The US launch comes in canned format via Whole Foods and Target, with each 8.4 fl oz can priced around $6 direct from the brand or $21.99 a 4-pack on Instacart. The premise is that a good Negroni doesn’t need to be Italian or alcohol-containing, just bitter-sweet with orange and a serious herbal lift, served over a single large ice cube rather than shaken.

Poured directly from the can, Lapo’s is sweet-heavy and the bitterness hides; poured over a single large ice cube with an orange slice and the dilution unlocks the structure, the bitter lifts, the orange peel gives the aroma the can lacks. It’s not VinePair’s Phony Negroni (closer to a real Negroni) but at $6 per serving it’s a credible 6 PM alternative and the Italian provenance is doing real work. Best Day Brewing’s near beer claims, St. Agrestis’ “drinks for recovering hedonists” line and Lapo’s Florentine frame share a single thesis: a Negroni ritual is a ritual no matter what the can says — and the best NA aperitivos are the ones that take that ritual seriously.

Tasting notes

Nose

Orange peel and bitter herbs with a citric lift; the Italian aperitivo family is in the room.

Palate

Bittersweet, with a Campari-style bitter-orange lift balanced by a sweeter vermouth-style edge; can be too sweet if poured flat without ice.

Finish

Bitter and a touch warm, the gentian-like lift carrying the close.

How to drink it

Always over a large ice cube; the can benefits from the dilution and from the chilling.

Best for: A 6 PM pour when Aperol doesn't suit and you want something closer to the bitter Italian side of the Negroni family.

Verdict

Florentine bartender's homage to the Negroni that drinks closer to the real thing than the Aperol-leaning cousins — but the can format asks the drinker to do a little work for it.
