Lyre's Italian Orange

Lyre's · Cocktails

A built-from-scratch blood-orange aperitif under 0.3% ABV. Useful in a Negroni-shaped drink. Not wine, not a spirit, not dealcoholized.

Dealcoholized: No

Product facts

82/100

Is it actually dealcoholized?

Dealcoholized: No — formulated as a zero-proof alternative

Product overview

Lyre’s does the industry a favor on its FAQ: the products are “purposefully crafted as non-alcoholic from the outset.” The same page draws the line this site exists to police. Dealcoholized drinks start as alcoholic drinks. Lyre’s starts as a recipe.

Italian Orange is therefore in our cocktail file as a control, not a hypocrisy. It is a blood-orange bitter designed to stand in for an Italian aperitivo in mixed drinks. The ingredient list is a food-lab list: water, sugar, fermented grape-juice concentrate, natural flavours, citric acid, caramel, preservatives, cellulose gum, Allura Red. There is no still story, no vacuum column, no “we took the Campari out of Campari.”

That is not an insult. A well-made formulation can be a better drink than a poorly dealcoholized one. It is an insult to the reader when a formulated aperitif borrows the language of alcohol removal. Lyre’s, here, does not.

Use it where you would use a bitter orange aperitif: ice, soda, orange slice; or as one-third of a zero-proof Negroni. Score it on those terms. Do not file it next to Noughty or Leitz. Those bottles were wine. This one never was.

Tasting notes

Nose

Blood orange, ruby grapefruit, and a candied cherry note. The pith is there. So is a slightly cosmetic citrus that never quite smells like a bitter made from a barrel.

Palate

Sweet-bitter and thick enough to mix. Orange oil, maraschino, and a dry peel finish. Cellulose gum is doing some of the grip. It works in soda and in a three-part cocktail; it is not a sipping amaro.

Finish

Pith and dye-bright citrus. Cleaner than a cheap grenadine, sweeter than Campari.

How to drink it

Over ice with soda and orange, or in a 1:1:1 cocktail with a gin alternative and a vermouth alternative. The producer says it is not intended neat. Agree.

Best for: Zero-proof Negroni builds, spritzes, and soda. Not a neat pour.

Verdict

A convincing bitter-orange mixer with no dealcoholization story to tell — and Lyre's is honest about that. Judge it as a formulated aperitif, not as stripped Campari.
