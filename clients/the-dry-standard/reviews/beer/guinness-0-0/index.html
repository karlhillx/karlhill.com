Guinness 0.0

Guinness · Beer · Cold filtration after a conventional Guinness brew

A St James's Gate stout with the alcohol filtered out cold. Cream, coffee, and that familiar surge — then a slightly sweeter, lighter finish.

Dealcoholized: Yes

Product facts

91/100

Is it actually dealcoholized?

Dealcoholized: Yes

Method: Cold filtration after a conventional Guinness brew

Base beverage: Stout brewed from water, barley, hops, and yeast at St James's Gate

Sources disagree on the following points. We do not pick a winner.

  • Abv: Guinness markets the beer as 0.0% alcohol. A detailed third-party review reports a maximum detectable trace around 0.05% ABV, the floor of common lab reporting.
  • Calories: Diageo's 2020 launch note cited 16 kcal/100 ml and 70 kcal per standard can. The current Guinness product page lists 17 kcal/100 ml.

Product overview

Guinness had a choice, and the brewery said so. Innovation brewer Aisling Ryan told The Irish Times they could brew a beer that never made much alcohol, or they could brew Guinness and take the alcohol out. They chose the second path. That is the entire editorial reason this can sits in our beer pile instead of the “NA-style” pile.

The removal step is cold filtration, not a heated strip. Guinness’s own language is careful: the process avoids thermal stress, then the beer is blended back into balance. The widget still rattles. The pour still surges. The liquid is still that particular ruby-black.

Read the ingredients. Fructose and “natural flavourings” are in the can. That does not make the beer formulated from scratch. It does mean the glass is not a chemically untouched Draught with ethanol subtracted and nothing added. Taste with that in mind and the slight confectionery mid-palate makes sense.

Calories are low either way — mid-60s to mid-70s per can, depending on which producer sheet you trust. We are not going to invent a single number when Diageo and Guinness.com are one calorie apart.

Tasting notes

Nose

Roasted barley, cocoa nib, and a little coffee grounds. Nitrogen does the usual theater. It smells like Guinness poured too early in the day, which is the compliment.

Palate

Silky, bitter-sweet, and shorter than Draught. The roast is genuine. A faint confectionery edge — fructose is on the label — keeps it from being a perfect carbon copy.

Finish

Dry cocoa and a clean, slightly thin close. The head lasts. The beer does not.

How to drink it

Cold, in a pint glass, poured to let the surge settle. Do not serve it like a lager.

Best for: A pub pint when the rest of the table is drinking the 4.2%. Also the best case for dealcoholized beer as a category.

Verdict

The rare dealcoholized beer that still pours, looks, and drinks like itself. Cold filtration keeps the roast; the fructose is the compromise you can taste if you hunt for it.
