Giesen 0% Rosé

Giesen · Wine · Spinning cone technology — aroma, alcohol, and body separated, then aroma and body recombined

Giesen ferments a full-strength Marlborough and Waipara rosé, then removes the alcohol with spinning cone technology. Less than 0.5% ABV.

Is it actually dealcoholized?

Dealcoholized: Yes

Method: Spinning cone technology — aroma, alcohol, and body separated, then aroma and body recombined

Base beverage: Full-strength New Zealand rosé from aromatic and red varietals, then dealcoholized

Product overview

Giesen does not change the story for the rosé. Fruit from Marlborough and Waipara is fermented as wine, then spinning cone technology splits aroma, alcohol, and body. Aroma and body go back together; a little grape juice restores mouthfeel. The producer lists under 0.5% ABV, 13 calories per 100 ml, and 18 g/L residual sugar.

The glass is the same argument as the 0% Riesling: varietal fruit survives the cone. Peach and strawberry, a dry-seeming finish, less weight than a 12% Waipara rosé. If you already trust this pipeline, this is the pink bottle in the set.

Tasting notes

Nose

White peach, apple, and fresh strawberry — lifted, not candied.

Palate

Cranberry and stone fruit over a light frame. 18 g/L residual sugar reads off-dry, not soda.

Finish

Crisp and short. The acidity does the work the ethanol used to.

How to drink it

Well chilled, white-wine glass.

Best for: Charcuterie, a warm afternoon, anyone who already likes the Giesen 0% whites.

Verdict

The same spinning-cone pipeline as the 0% Sauvignon Blanc and Riesling, applied to a pale New Zealand rosé. White peach and strawberry, dry enough for lunch.
