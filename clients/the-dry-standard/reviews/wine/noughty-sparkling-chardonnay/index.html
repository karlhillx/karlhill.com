Wine

Thomson & Scott Noughty Sparkling Chardonnay

Organic La Mancha Chardonnay, dealcoholized, then lightly carbonated. Pale, crisp, and modestly sweet — with a documented alcohol-removal step.

Dealcoholized: Yes

84/100

Is it actually dealcoholized?

Dealcoholized: Yes

Method: Vacuum distillation with aroma recovery; some trade sheets also name spinning-cone technology

Base beverage: Organically grown Spanish Chardonnay, fermented in stainless steel, then carbonated after alcohol removal

Sources disagree on the following points. We do not pick a winner.

  • Method: Thomson & Scott's own product page describes de-alcoholisation by vacuum distillation with aroma recovery, then light carbonation. Some trade listings additionally mention spinning-cone technology before or alongside vacuum distillation. We print both and do not collapse them into one proprietary story.
  • Abv: Producer technical data list 0.0% vol. Several US retailers describe the wine as under 0.5% ABV, which matches American non-alcoholic labeling rather than a lab contradiction we can resolve from here.

Product overview

Noughty starts as wine. That is the sentence most NA sparkling labels will not earn. Amanda Thomson’s project ferments organic Chardonnay from sandy, calcareous vineyards in southern Spain, then removes the alcohol and adds a gentle sparkle. The bottle is not méthode traditionnelle. It is a still wine that lost its ethanol and gained carbonation.

The method language is almost, but not quite, consistent. The producer page says vacuum distillation with aroma recovery. Importer and trade copy often add Carl Jung’s vacuum technique and, in at least one technical sheet, spinning-cone technology. Those can describe adjacent low-temperature alcohol-removal tools. They are not identical sentences. Until Thomson & Scott publishes a single process diagram, we treat the dealcoholization as verified and the exact hardware mix as incompletely specified.

What you can trust in the glass: pale color, apple, a measured sweetness (2.9 g/100 ml), and 14 kcal/100 ml. What you should not expect: autolytic depth, a dry brut finish, or the weight of a 12% blanc de blancs. For a toast, that trade is acceptable. For a cellar conversation about sparkling wine, say the quiet part: this is dealcoholized Chardonnay with bubbles added, and that is still rarer — and more honest — than most of the category.

Tasting notes

Nose

Ripe apple, a little lemon peel, and a faint pastry note that never quite becomes brioche. The bubbles do more work than the aroma.

Palate

Brisk, lightly sweet, and simpler than a bottle-fermented wine. Green apple and citrus sit on a slender body. The mousse is fine enough for a toast.

Finish

Short, clean, and a touch sugary if the glass sits. Serve it colder than you think.

How to drink it

Well chilled, flutes or white-wine stems. Fine with salty snacks; less happy next to richly sauced food.

Best for: Aperitif service, a toast, or a spritz base when you want wine rather than soda.

Verdict

A clean, apple-forward sparkling wine that was actually a wine first. It will not replace grower Champagne. It will replace most of the NA 'bubbly' that never fermented.
