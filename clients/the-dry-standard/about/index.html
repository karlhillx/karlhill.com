About The Dry Standard

Why this site exists, how we tell dealcoholized drinks from formulated ones, and how the scores work.

The Dry Standard is an editorial review site for beverages at 0.5% ABV or less. We are especially interested in drinks that were fermented or distilled first and then had the alcohol taken out.

That is a narrower brief than “non-alcoholic.” It is also the point.

What we cover

Dealcoholized wine, beer, spirits, aperitifs, cider, sparkling wine, and ready-to-drink cocktails or spritzes. A product can appear here if it meets the ABV ceiling. It will not be called dealcoholized unless we can show that alcohol was removed from a finished, or at least high-proof, alcoholic liquid.

The distinction we will not blur

Dealcoholized means a documented alcohol-removal step: vacuum distillation, spinning cone, reverse osmosis, membrane or cold filtration, reverse distillation, or another named process.

Formulated / zero-proof means the drink was built to taste like beer, wine, or a spirit without that step. Botanicals, flavors, juices, and thickeners are legitimate tools. They are not dealcoholization.

Not verified means the producer has not published a method we can cite. We do not infer one from a “0.0” on the label.

If sources disagree, we print the disagreement.

What we will not do

  • Invent ABV, ingredients, origin, price, or a production method.
  • Call a product dealcoholized because it is marketed as non-alcoholic.
  • Preach sobriety, wellness, or “clean” living.
  • Publish a review to hit a quota when the facts are thin.
  • Stuff keywords into copy that should read like a magazine.

Tasting notes are opinion. Production facts are sourced or omitted.

The 100-point scale

The number is a quality score, not a likeness-to-ethanol score. A formulated aperitif can outscore a clumsy dealcoholized wine.

We weigh:

Factor What we are asking
Flavor Is it good to drink?
Aroma Does the nose have character, or only sweetness?
Texture / body Does it have weight without gum-and-syrup tricks doing all the work?
Finish Does it end, or collapse?
Complexity More than one idea in the glass?
Balance Sugar, acid, bitterness, carbonation — in proportion?
Value Is the price honest for what you get?
Authenticity to category Does a stout still behave like a stout?

Bands we use internally:

  • 90–100 — Exceptional. We would reorder it.
  • 80–89 — Excellent. Worth seeking out.
  • 70–79 — Recommended, with caveats.
  • 60–69 — Adequate. There is a reason to skip it.
  • Below 60 — Not recommended.

Methodology lives with the reviews, not in a PDF nobody reads.

How reviews get published

Products enter a queue. They are researched, drafted, and checked against sources. A review that fails factual validation does not publish, even if the calendar says it is a publishing day. The cadence is configurable — by default, three reviews a week — and it is a ceiling, not a demand.

Local models do the drafting work where that is practical. They do not get to invent a spinning cone.

What this is not

This is not a shop. There are no carts, no accounts, and no affiliate obligation in the first phase. Purchase links are citations of where a bottle has been sold in the United States, not endorsements of a retailer.
