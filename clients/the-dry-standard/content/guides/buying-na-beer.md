The beer aisle is where production type stops being pedantry and starts saving you money. A 0.0 can be a full-strength recipe with the alcohol taken out. It can be a beer that never climbed past 0.5%. It can also be flavored water with hops on the label. All three show up next to each other.

## Decide what you want the glass to do

If you want a pub pour that still behaves like beer — roast, bitterness, head — start with dealcoholized stubs of known brands and with low-fermentation craft that publishes a method. If you want citrus and chill without malt weight, hop water and RTDs are a different category. Do not grade one against the other.

For the process fork itself, see [dealcoholized beer versus beer brewed to 0.5%](dealcoholized-beer-vs-na-beer/).

## Read for a named process, not a lifestyle claim

Words that help: cold filtration, membrane, vacuum, reverse osmosis, arrested fermentation, specialized yeast. Words that do not: "crafted for mindful drinking," "zero compromise," "brewed differently." A brand that will not say how the alcohol left — or never arrived — leaves you guessing.

Athletic-style proprietary NA brewing can be excellent. Treat it as naturally low alcohol until they document a removal step. Guinness-style cold filtration is dealcoholized when the producer says so with a source you can cite.

## Taste for bitterness and dry finish, not for ethanol heat

Ethanol is gone. Body has to come from malt, protein, nitrogen, or residual sugar. A stout that finishes dry and bitter is doing the category's work. An IPA that collapses into hop tea after two sips is not saved by a loud aroma. Fructose and lactose show up often; they are patches, not disqualifiers, but you should know they are there.

## Shop cold, then rotate stock

NA beer stales. Prefer a shop with a cold box and a date code you can read. Multipacks of a beer you already like beat a mixed case of strangers. Once you have a house lager, pils, and IPA that hold up, expand by style — not by whatever is new on the endcap.

## Use the cellar filters

On this site, filter beer by production type and method first, then by style. Score alone will not tell you whether you wanted a filtered stout or a hop seltzer. The badge and the number answer different questions; buy with both in view.
