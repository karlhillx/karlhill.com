Non-alcoholic is a legal bucket. In the United States it usually means under 0.5% alcohol by volume. Dealcoholized is a manufacturing claim. One is a ceiling. The other is a history.

A dealcoholized Riesling was wine: grapes, yeast, a fermentation that produced ethanol, then a machine that took the ethanol out. A zero-proof gin alternative may never have been gin. It can still be a good drink. It is a different object.

## Why the distinction matters

Alcohol is a solvent and a texture. Removing it from a finished beer or wine leaves a hole. Producers fill that hole with residual sugar, grape concentrate, carbonation, oak extract, or nothing at all. You can taste the hole. You can also taste whether there was ever a wine or beer there to begin with.

Formulated products skip the hole. They design sweetness, bitterness, and mouthfeel from food-grade parts. The best of them are honest mixers. The worst borrow vineyard language they did not earn.

## How to read a label in thirty seconds

1. Does the producer name an alcohol-removal method?
2. Do they describe a base wine, beer, or spirit that existed before that step?
3. Is the ingredient list short and agricultural, or long and industrial?
4. If you cannot answer those questions from a primary source, the honest badge is **not verified**.

## What we print on every review

**Production type: Dealcoholized** — we can cite the removal step.

**Production type: Alternative** — built as a non-alcoholic analogue from the start.

**Production type: Naturally low alcohol** — fermented or otherwise traditionally produced, finishing at ≤0.5% ABV without a removal step.

**Production type: Hybrid** — dealcoholized material plus other defining non-alcoholic ingredients, or more than one production approach.

**Production type: Not verified** — the marketing is NA; the factory floor is unpublished.

Verification sits on its own line. We would rather under-claim than decorate a bottle with a spinning cone we have not seen.
