Josef Leitz's Eins-Zwei-Zero line is the same spinning cone process that the Riesling review on this desk documents — conventionally vinified Rheingau wine, gently dealcoholized, with the varietal character preserved by the low-temperature process. The Sparkling Rosé applies that to a Rheingau rosé base, with 0.0% ABV and the same producer discipline.

The glass shows wild strawberry and raspberry over a floral lift, with fine bubbles and a drier palate than most NA sparkling rosés manage — Leitz's winemaking shows in the restraint. It won't replace a real Champagne rosé, and it's not trying to. As the zero-proof sparkling for the rosé occasion, it's one of the best executions in the category, and the Leitz name carries the credibility that the process deserves.
