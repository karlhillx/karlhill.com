The grape alone makes Lot 230 interesting: Souvignier Gris, a modern Piwi hybrid bred in Germany for disease resistance and increasingly planted across Europe for organic-friendly viticulture. 90+ Cellars makes it traditionally in the South of France, then removes the alcohol through reverse osmosis — the producer's own site is explicit about the method, which gives them a sparkling NA that's genuinely cheap to produce at the source and confirmed as a real dealcoholized wine rather than a formulated one. It is the lowest priced dealcoholized sparkling on a US shelf that isn't a relabeled bulk gallon, and for $13, that's a credential worth filing.

The drink itself is what you'd expect from a clean-brut Souvignier Gris: green-apple and lemon, a faint bready note from the secondary fermentation, a crisp, brief finish. Compared to a Mionetto or a French Bloom it lacks the varietal fingerprint of a real Prosecco or Chardonnay — Souvignier Gris is a workhorse grape, not a showpiece — but it does its job in a flute, with a glass of something beside it (oyster, fritto misto, an afternoon on a porch). The cheapest credible pour in the NA sparkling category, and the friendliest entry point for anyone who wants to know if this whole aisle is for them.
