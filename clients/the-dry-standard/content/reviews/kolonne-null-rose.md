Kolonne Null is one of the few producers in this category with a lab you could visit: their dealcoholization happens in-house in Berlin, and their own site documents vacuum distillation at roughly 30°C as the method — low enough temperature that the aromatic compounds survive the process intact. The fruit is Provence rosé, vinified conventionally to full strength first. That combination, plus a €10.70 shelf price that undercuts nearly every equivalent French NA rosé, makes this one of the more defensible bottles in the category.

What lands in the glass is dry and lean rather than fruity-sweet: strawberry and white peach over a stony mineral spine, a brief citrus close. It's noticeably lighter in body than a real Provence rosé — removing the ethanol removes the weight — but the varietal character survived the vacuum step, which is the whole point of doing it cold. For a desk wine, this is the honest middle of the range: better than the sweet, thin dealcoholized rosés that dominate the budget tier, short of the spinning-cone whites at the top.
