Nonny makes its Czech Pilsner from four ingredients — water, barley, hops, yeast — and gets it to 42 calories a can, which is remarkable discipline for a beer that actually tastes like beer. Worth a correction up front: this is a Canadian beer, brewed in Vancouver, BC, not a Czech import — "Czech Pilsner" describes the style Nonny is brewing to, in the tradition of Saaz hops and a crisp, golden lager, not the country of origin.

Nonny's own site is consistent across every page: "always below 0.5% alcohol," with no removal-process language anywhere, which reads as formulated rather than dealcoholized. The hop bitterness is right (grassy, present, not apologetic), the malt is clean, and the finish is dry rather than sweet — four ingredients, 42 calories, and a bitterness that suggests the brewers drank a lot of real Pilsner Urquell while developing it. As a light, honest lager, it earns its place on the shelf.
